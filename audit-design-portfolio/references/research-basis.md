## How to use this reference

This file records the public product patterns and hiring guidance that informed the framework. Use it to maintain or explain the skill. Do not copy proprietary rubrics, marketing claims, scores, or report language.

Sources may change. Verify current source content before making claims about a product.

## Portfolio audit products

### Evalv

Source: [Evalv features](https://www.evalv.ai/features) and [demo report](https://www.evalv.ai/demo-report)

Observed product patterns:

- Role-specific portfolio criteria
- Portfolio and resume comparison against a JD
- Visual, hierarchy, storytelling, and section-level analysis
- Recruiter-oriented scanability and positioning lens
- Priority, secondary, and nice-to-have actions
- Website technical checks such as loading or failed assets
- Page and project breakdowns

Adopt:

- JD-to-evidence analysis
- Section-specific findings
- Recruiter lens
- Prioritized action plan
- Technical-health checks when tools support them
- A report hierarchy that moves from diagnosis to page-level evidence and actions

Reject or modify:

- Replace scores and percentages with evidence grades.
- Do not estimate seniority as fact.
- Do not prescribe fabricated metrics.

### Portfolio Feedback

Source: [Portfolio Feedback](https://portfoliofeedback.com/)

Observed product patterns:

- Visual annotations attached to portfolio pages
- Content, structure, and layout feedback
- Emphasis on actionable and private review

Adopt:

- Locate feedback by screenshot, page, section, heading, or visible element.
- Treat portfolio material as sensitive user data.
- Keep the reviewed page and its feedback in the same visual field.

### Refine

Source: [Refine](https://refinedesign.me/)

Observed product patterns:

- Multi-dimensional review
- Strengths, blind spots, and ranked actions
- Re-scan after revisions
- Portfolio-level and screenshot-level analysis

Adopt:

- Multi-dimensional deep review
- Prioritized recommendations
- Re-audit and change tracking
- Preserve stable finding references across revision cycles.

Reject or modify:

- Do not use an overall 0–100 score, traffic-light performance band, or cross-candidate benchmark.

### FolioReview

Source: [FolioReview](https://folioreview.io/)

Observed product patterns:

- Automatic role detection across UX, UI, Research, Service, and Writing
- Structured review using clarity, impact, consistency, narrative, hierarchy, and role relevance
- Action plan and repeat evaluations

Adopt:

- Infer a role lens when needed, but tell the user and allow correction.
- Change criteria by discipline.
- Produce a reusable report and support re-audit.

Reject or modify:

- Do not frame improvement as reaching a perfect score.

## Human review services

### ADPList Portfolio Review

Source: [ADPList Portfolio Review](https://adplist.org/services/portfolio-review)

Transferable guidance:

- Review case-study structure, visual quality, narrative, process, and target-role evidence.
- Encourage the candidate to bring two or three focused questions.
- End with a clear action plan.
- Look for thinking, ownership, and impact rather than final pixels alone.

Encoded decisions:

- Accept one to three optional focus questions.
- Provide concrete, prioritized actions.
- Keep the audit conversational and suitable for follow-up.

## Hiring-team guidance

### Indeed Design hiring managers

Source: [UX Design Portfolio Advice from Hiring Managers](https://indeed.design/article/ux-design-portfolio-advice-from-hiring-managers/)

Transferable guidance:

- Show the full story: problem, role, team, process, constraints, iteration, and result.
- Final mockups without reasoning are insufficient.
- Real-world shipped work exposes constraints that speculative work may not.
- Surface a candidate's strengths without making every project identical.
- Calibrate entry-level candidates for transferable skills and potential.
- Check polish, access, and password friction.
- Avoid formulaic process narratives that hide shallow understanding.

Encoded decisions:

- Separate project status and evidence strength.
- Audit iteration and rationale.
- Include student and career-changer calibration.
- Include access and basic-polish checks.

### Audible portfolio review guidance

Source: [Audible Portfolio Review Guidelines](https://tbcdn.talentbrew.com/company/75/cms/audible/pdf/ux_page/portfolio_review_guidelines_2024.pdf)

Transferable guidance:

- Make goals, intended outcomes, and success evidence visible.
- Show early iterations and final implemented work.
- Explain candidate ownership explicitly.
- Connect design choices to user, business, technical, legal, compliance, and brand constraints.
- Show impediments, outcomes, reflection, and learning.
- Adapt material to the presentation context rather than reusing an online portfolio unchanged.

Encoded decisions:

- Audit ownership, constraints, iteration, outcome, and reflection.
- Distinguish standalone portfolio, PDF, and live presentation use.

### U.S. Digital Response UX hiring guide

Source: [User Experience Hiring Guide](https://digitalgovernmenthub.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/11/User-Experience-Hiring-Guide.pdf)

Transferable guidance:

- Use structured, role-related review criteria.
- For researchers, examine ownership, method judgment, stakeholder collaboration, synthesis, impact, accessibility, and inclusion.
- Distinguish weak mentions from applied evidence.

Encoded decisions:

- Include accessibility and inclusion in role lenses.
- Use evidence grades rather than a numerical hiring rubric.
- Avoid gut-feel or cultural-similarity evaluation.

## Role-specific guidance

### UX Research

Source: [A practical rubric template for reviewing UX Research portfolios](https://depth.drillbitlabs.com/p/uxr-portfolio-rubric)

Transferable dimensions:

- Clarity
- Rigor
- Impact
- Engagement
- Growth

Additional lessons:

- Start from a target role or representative JD.
- Explain why methods were selected, not merely which methods were used.
- Balance sufficient detail with scanability.
- Show reflection, adaptation, and learning.
- Adjust expectations across career stages.

Encoded decisions:

- Use the five dimensions as qualitative evidence areas.
- Do not copy its numerical scoring.
- Require method rationale, limitations, influence, and growth.

### Content Design

Source: [What We Look for in Content Design Portfolios and Work Samples](https://product.hubspot.com/blog/how-to-content-design-portfolios-and-work-samples)

Transferable guidance:

- Clarity matters more than an expensive or elaborate presentation.
- Two or three representative projects may be sufficient.
- Focus on product experiences for product-content roles.
- Explain the problem, evidence, goal, role, process, and outcome.
- Show rationale, end-to-end thinking, iteration, constraints, and tradeoffs.
- Relevant work may include accessibility, content audits, governance, modeling, testing, taxonomy, localization, onboarding, errors, forms, and product flows.
- Respect NDAs and consider public, permitted, volunteer, or side-project alternatives.

Encoded decisions:

- Use a dedicated content-design lens.
- Accept multiple portfolio formats.
- Audit product-content reasoning rather than final wording alone.
- Include NDA-aware limitations and alternatives.

## Decisions encoded in this skill

This skill combines the strongest transferable mechanisms:

1. Complete portfolio inventory and cross-case synthesis before project-level advice.
2. Two-pass review: fast-screen and deep-review.
3. Three lenses: recruiter, hiring manager, and craft.
4. JD capability model before portfolio judgment.
5. Exact evidence locations, visual callouts, and section-level findings.
6. Role-specific criteria and career-stage calibration.
7. Evidence grades without scores or percentage matches.
8. Portfolio experience and technical-health checks when available.
9. Prioritized actions and concrete suggestion structures.
10. Read-only behavior and explicit candidate choice.
11. Anti-fabrication rules for metrics, ownership, research, and outcomes.
12. Re-audit that distinguishes real evidence improvement from cosmetic rewriting.
13. Privacy, NDA, accessibility, inclusion, and anti-bias safeguards.
14. An artifact-first visual report that moves from executive diagnosis to annotated evidence, detailed findings, and prioritized actions.
15. Captured website and rendered-PDF views with stable HTML overlay pins rather than modifications to the original artifact.
16. A short chat handoff that links to the reusable report instead of presenting the full audit as a conversation transcript.
