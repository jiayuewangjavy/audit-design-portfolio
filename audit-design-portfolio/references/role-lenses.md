## Selecting a lens

Select the primary lens from the JD responsibilities, not title alone. Add a secondary lens only when the JD genuinely combines disciplines.

Tell the user which lens was selected and why. Let the user correct an inferred role.

Apply the common framework to every role, then use the role-specific expectations below. Do not demand equal depth in every discipline.

## UI/UX Designer

### Look for

- User and task understanding
- Information architecture and interaction flows
- Wireframes, prototypes, and meaningful iteration
- Visual hierarchy, layout, typography, color, and component consistency
- Usability evaluation and response to findings
- Accessibility considerations
- Responsive and multi-state behavior
- Clear connection between problem, decisions, and final experience
- Personal ownership and collaboration

### Strong evidence often includes

- Before/after or problem/solution comparisons with rationale
- Important states, errors, edge cases, and responsive behavior
- Iterations linked to feedback or constraints
- Explanation of how visual choices support use
- Shipped or tested evidence when available

### Avoid false proxies

- Do not treat a design-process diagram as proof of user-centered practice.
- Do not equate fashionable visual style with usability.
- Do not require every standard UX artifact.
- Do not penalize limited research access when constraints and compensating methods are honest.

## Product Designer

### Look for

- Product and user problem framing
- Business, user, technical, legal, or operational constraints
- Prioritization and tradeoffs
- End-to-end interaction and visual craft
- Collaboration with product, engineering, research, data, content, and stakeholders
- Iteration and decision rationale
- Outcomes and learning
- Level-appropriate product strategy, systems thinking, and influence

### Strong evidence often includes

- A clear decision the candidate personally drove
- Alternatives considered and why one direction won
- Scoping or sequencing decisions
- Connection between research or data and product change
- Evidence of shipped behavior, adoption, quality, efficiency, or a decision enabled
- For senior roles, influence across teams or over time

### Avoid false proxies

- Do not reward a long generic design-thinking narrative.
- Do not require revenue metrics for every project.
- Do not treat a design system alone as product strategy.
- Do not infer leadership from frequent use of `we led`.

## UX Researcher or Product Researcher

### Look for

- Research question and decision context
- Method selection and rationale
- Sampling, recruitment, ethics, bias, and limitations
- Study design and execution
- Analysis and synthesis
- Evidence quality and rigor appropriate to the question
- Communication and stakeholder collaboration
- How findings informed product, service, or organizational decisions
- Reflection, adaptation, and growth
- Research operations or strategic scope when required by senior roles

### Strong evidence often includes

- Why a method fit the decision and constraints
- How the researcher handled imperfect recruitment or data
- Traceability from evidence to insight to recommendation
- How stakeholders engaged with findings
- A product or organizational decision changed, clarified, or de-risked
- Transparent limitations and what would be done next

### Avoid false proxies

- Do not judge rigor by the number of methods listed.
- Do not require confidential raw data.
- Do not equate an attractive report with valid research.
- Do not assume findings caused business outcomes without evidence.
- Do not penalize academic experience automatically; assess transfer to product decisions.

Use the cross-cutting dimensions `Clarity`, `Rigor`, `Impact`, `Engagement`, and `Growth`, but express them as evidence grades rather than scores.

## Content Designer or UX Writer

### Look for

- Product problem and end-to-end experience, not isolated copy
- Evidence that content was needed
- Content strategy and rationale
- Information architecture, taxonomy, naming, or content modeling when relevant
- Voice, tone, terminology, and consistency
- Inclusive language, accessibility, localization, and internationalization
- Content testing and iteration
- Collaboration and influence
- Governance, standards, or scalable content systems for senior roles
- Outcomes for users, product, operations, or the organization

### Strong evidence often includes

- The original experience and why its content failed
- Alternative content directions and decision criteria
- How content changed interaction or comprehension
- Testing or evidence behind language choices
- Error, empty, onboarding, notification, form, search, or settings states in context
- How the candidate influenced the whole experience rather than filling copy slots

### Avoid false proxies

- Do not prioritize portfolio visual spectacle over clarity.
- Do not treat marketing articles as equivalent to product content unless the JD asks for them.
- Do not judge only the final words; inspect problem framing, systems, and rationale.
- Do not require a custom website. Clear PDFs, documents, decks, and design files can be valid work samples.

## UX Engineer

### Look for

- Translation between design intent and technical implementation
- Prototyping fidelity appropriate to the question
- Interaction behavior, states, and edge cases
- Component architecture and reusable patterns
- Accessibility and semantic implementation
- Performance and feasibility reasoning
- Collaboration with design and engineering
- Validation with users or product teams where relevant
- Documentation and handoff improvements

### Strong evidence often includes

- A working prototype or implementation with inspectable behavior
- Explanation of technical choices and tradeoffs
- How code or prototyping changed a product decision
- Keyboard, focus, screen-reader, reduced-motion, or responsive considerations
- Reusable components with clear usage constraints

### Avoid false proxies

- Do not judge only screenshot aesthetics.
- Do not require production-scale code for every prototype.
- Do not equate framework popularity with engineering quality.
- Do not ignore design judgment because the artifact is code-heavy.

## Design Engineer

### Look for

- High-quality visual and interaction implementation
- Ability to design and build
- Motion, responsiveness, and state behavior with purpose
- Component and design-system thinking
- Accessibility and performance
- Technical experimentation and prototyping
- Collaboration and influence across design and engineering
- Production quality, maintainability, and constraints
- Clear explanation of what was personally designed versus implemented

### Strong evidence often includes

- Live or recorded interaction with a clear purpose
- Code, component, or system excerpts tied to user experience
- Deliberate tradeoffs among fidelity, performance, accessibility, and maintainability
- Tooling or patterns that raised team quality
- For senior roles, systems or platform-level leverage

### Avoid false proxies

- Do not reward animation simply for being elaborate.
- Do not assume custom-coded means usable or maintainable.
- Do not judge engineering solely by visible UI.
- Do not judge design solely by implementation novelty.

## Hybrid and ambiguous roles

When a JD mixes roles:

1. Identify the primary outcome the company is hiring for.
2. Select one primary lens.
3. Select at most one secondary lens unless the user asks for a broad audit.
4. Show which requirements belong to each lens.
5. Flag when the JD appears to combine multiple full-time disciplines unrealistically.

Examples:

- `Product Designer, Design Systems`: Product Designer primary; Design Engineer or UI/UX secondary depending on responsibilities.
- `UX Researcher/Designer`: Use the discipline that owns the main decisions as primary.
- `Content Designer with research`: Content Designer primary; apply research criteria only to expected content research.
